Nutrition

You Can't Out-Train
a Bad Diet

📅 16 September 2025 · 7 min read · The Workout Warehouse

You're training five days a week. You're consistent, you're pushing hard, and you're showing up even when you don't feel like it. But the results aren't matching the effort. Sound familiar?

The answer is almost always in the kitchen. Not because exercise doesn't matter — it does — but because nutrition is the multiplier. Get it right, and everything you do in the gym compounds. Get it wrong, and you're fighting against yourself every single session.

01. Protein Is Non-Negotiable

Your muscles are built from protein. When you train, you break muscle tissue down. When you recover — and eat enough protein — it rebuilds stronger. Without sufficient protein, that rebuild doesn't happen properly.

Aim for 1.6 to 2.2g of protein per kilogram of bodyweight daily. For a 75kg person, that's roughly 120–165g. Spread across three to four meals, that's very achievable — chicken, eggs, tuna, Greek yoghurt, cottage cheese, and legumes are your friends.

02. Calories Are Information, Not the Enemy

The most common mistake people make is seeing food as something to be feared or restricted. Calories are energy. They tell your body what to do. Too few and your body breaks down muscle for fuel, slows your metabolism, and leaves you exhausted. Too many and the surplus gets stored.

The goal is to understand roughly what you need — not to obsess over every gram, but to have enough awareness that you're not wildly over or under. For most people training four to five times a week, that's somewhere between 2,000 and 2,800 calories depending on size, gender, and goals.

03. Carbohydrates Fuel Your Training

Low-carb diets are everywhere. And for sedentary people trying to lose weight, they can work. But if you're training hard — lifting, running, doing classes — cutting carbs will kill your performance.

Carbohydrates are your body's preferred fuel source for high-intensity work. Without them, your sessions suffer, your recovery slows, and your results stall. Focus on quality sources: oats, rice, sweet potato, fruit, and whole grains. Time them around your training for best effect.

04. Meal Timing Matters More Than You Think

You don't need to eat within 30 seconds of finishing a set, but nutrition timing does play a role. A meal or snack containing protein and carbohydrates within an hour or two of training helps kickstart recovery and replenish what you've used.

Equally, training fasted — especially for longer sessions — often means your body turns to muscle for fuel before fat. That's the opposite of what most people want. A light pre-workout meal or protein shake can make a noticeable difference to both performance and results.

05. Hydration Is a Performance Variable

A 2% drop in hydration can reduce performance by up to 10%. Most people walk into the gym mildly dehydrated and don't even know it.

Water isn't just for thirst — it regulates temperature, transports nutrients, lubricates joints, and supports every metabolic process in your body. Drink consistently throughout the day, not just during training. Two to three litres is a reasonable daily baseline for active individuals.

06. Keep It Simple and Sustainable

The best diet is the one you can actually follow. Extreme meal plans, cutting out entire food groups, or surviving on chicken and broccoli for eight weeks might produce short-term results — but they're not sustainable, and anything you lose will come back when you stop.

Instead: eat mostly whole foods, hit your protein target, don't fear carbs, limit ultra-processed food, and allow yourself flexibility. Progress compounds over months and years — not two-week challenges.

The gym is where you create the stimulus for change. Nutrition is where the change actually happens. Respect both halves of the equation.
